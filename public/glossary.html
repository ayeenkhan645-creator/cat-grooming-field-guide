Coat-care vocabulary

Grooming terms, explained.

Short definitions of the language you will encounter in the guides.

Undercoat

A layer of shorter or finer hairs beneath longer protective guard hairs, present to varying degrees in different cats. Density and texture matter more for care than a breed name.

Guard hairs

Usually the longer, more visible outer hairs that contribute to the appearance and protection of the coat.

Matting

Clumps of tangled hair that tighten, often near areas of friction. Severe mats can cause pain and require professional removal.

Deshedding

Using grooming tools to collect already-loose hair. Aggressive techniques may irritate skin or damage healthy fur.

Quick

The living tissue within a claw. Cutting into it causes pain and bleeding, which is why conservative nail trimming and proper instruction matter.

Cooperative care

A way of teaching an animal to voluntarily participate in low-stress handling, with predictable cues, pauses and the ability to end the interaction.

Consent signal

Observable behavior indicating willingness to approach or remain engaged, such as returning after a brief pause. No single behavior is a universal guarantee of comfort.

Overgrooming

Excessive self-licking or hair removal that may reflect discomfort, stress or a medical problem. The term describes the pattern, not its cause.

Frict​ion area

A place where fur repeatedly rubs against itself or an object, such as behind the ears, under the forelegs or around a harness, making tangles more likely.

Cat Grooming Field Guide

Kind, practical coat-care education for people who share a home with a cat. Every cat is an individual; grooming is never a reason to override a clear refusal.
